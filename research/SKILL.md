# Research Toolkit

Answer the research question with evidence the reader can verify. Use the language and format the user requests. Match the depth of the work to the question and its stakes.

## Scope and investigate

1. Identify the question, audience, decision, scope, and observation cutoff. State assumptions that would change the answer. Ask when an unresolved ambiguity would materially change the study.
2. Read [the research workflow](references/workflow.md) for source selection, comparison, quantitative analysis, and reproducibility. Define eligibility, measures, and comparison rules before collecting cases or calculating results. For market event studies or bonds, also read [the financial methods](references/finance.md).
3. Prefer primary evidence suited to each claim. Open the underlying publication, documentation, or dataset. Track dates, coverage, definitions, and transformations. Distinguish observed values, calculations, scenario inputs, and interpretation.
4. Check the claims that drive the conclusion against the underlying sources. Reconcile counts, units, and results across text, tables, and charts. Report conflicts, exclusions, and limitations that change the interpretation.

## Write and verify

Lead with the finding and its scope. Explain how the evidence supports it, then make the methods and sources easy to inspect. Use tables for exact comparisons and charts for patterns. During revisions, keep figures that carry evidence.

For PDF reports, read [the report layout](references/layout.md). Use the bundled vector PDF helpers when they fit. A compact report can use three pages for findings, interpretation, and supporting evidence. Adjust the length to the task.

Read [the review checklist](references/review.md) before delivering. Check the actual links, calculations, and output. When a question cannot be answered from the available evidence, identify the missing evidence and how it limits the conclusion.
