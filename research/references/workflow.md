# Research workflow

## Choose the evidence

Use the original paper and supplements for a scientific result, the dataset and its documentation for a statistic, and the official publication or product documentation for a rule or specification. Follow summaries to the underlying source. Use independent sources to resolve meaningful disagreements and record which differences in time, population, definitions, or methods account for the disagreement. Cite the source directly beside the claim.

For a literature review, define the question, the populations or settings of interest, and the inclusion criteria. Record the search sources and dates when completeness matters. Compare designs, outcome definitions, and limitations before combining studies. Use a narrative synthesis when the measures or settings cannot reasonably be pooled.

For comparisons, use the same criteria and comparable periods across options. Identify tradeoffs relevant to the user's decision. Mark missing information and describe the limits of proxies.

## Analyze and record

Before a quantitative study, specify units, population or cases, inclusion and exclusion rules, outcome, baseline, and handling of missing observations. Preserve source observations and record each transformation. Calculate locally from machine readable data where possible. Keep denominators visible and report typical outcomes, spread, and adverse cases where useful. For time dependent studies, use information that would have been available at the stated decision time.

Check a typical case and a consequential outlier from raw observations. Verify that charts, tables, and text use the same cases and definitions. Use sensitivity checks for assumptions that materially affect the result and explain what changes.

For work that is costly to reproduce, keep a compact bundle with source URLs, retrieval dates, an observation cutoff, raw data where permitted, exclusions, calculations, and outputs. Share the bundle when requested and omit unrelated private information.
